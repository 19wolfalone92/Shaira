# SHAIRA v0.8 — A real staircase from absolutely zero Russian

**Student:** Adult beginner. All guidance and feedback are in **English**, because no Russian knowledge, Russian alphabet literacy, Russian keyboard proficiency or grammar is assumed.

## The problem this solves

Before this change, the guided course opened on a day containing a 10-vowel lesson, a six-word theme, a grammar multiple-choice question, a typed Russian spelling exercise and a speaking drill. That is too much at once for someone who has never seen Russian Cyrillic.

Now, for a new learner, the **Today** screen starts with an earlier foundation route of **78 tiny sequential steps**, in front of—not in place of—the existing 210 multi-skill study sessions.

### The new learning ladder

| Segment | Number | What a learner practices |
|---|---:|---|
| One Cyrillic letter at a time | 33 | Uppercase form, lowercase form, main sound with English cue, optional speech and finger tracing |
| Two-letter blends | 11 | Join only previously introduced letters into a pronounceable syllable |
| First short words | 11 | Decode words entirely formed from previously taught letters and connect them to English meanings |
| Tiny repeat-and-remember checks | 11 | Recall the previous letters, syllables and words without a big exam |
| Everyday phrase rungs | 12 | Recognise one meaningful full sentence or social expression per step |
| **Total** | **78** | Small steps over as many sessions and days as needed |

Each of the first 11 chapters adds exactly **three individual letters**, then one blend, one short word and one review. Example first six steps:

1. А / а — first sound and letter
2. М / м — a second letter
3. О / о — a third letter
4. ма — join two known sounds
5. мама — read and understand a real tiny word
6. Review: мама and ма

Only after the 33 Cyrillic letters and the little words does the student begin the 12 useful beginner phrases: «Это мама», «Меня зовут Шаира», «Привет», «Здравствуйте», «Спасибо», «Я дома», «Я учу русский», etc.

At no stage is a new word required to be decoded with letters that have **not yet been introduced**. All 33 Cyrillic letters, including Ё, Ы, Ь and Ъ, are covered individually. Unintroduced letters may appear only as *incorrect choice shapes* in the very first recognition tests; the learner does not need to know their pronunciation.

### A tiny step is not a calendar day

A step takes roughly **2–5 minutes**. Shaira can do one small step today, return for another tomorrow, or complete several when she feels confident. There are no mandatory streaks and no punishment for repetition. Progress is saved on the phone.

## Difficulty is always Shaira's choice

Difficulty selector appears directly on the tiny-step screen, with three explicit options:

| Mode | Required check | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **Gentle** (default) | Two simple recognition/matching choices; **no Russian typing required** | Starting from absolute zero |
| **Steady** | Two recognition checks plus one short Cyrillic typed answer | When recognition feels easy |
| **Challenge** | Four questions, including two typed Russian answers | When Shaira wants a real stretch |

Changing difficulty resets only the **unfinished check**, never completed rungs. A Russian on-screen keyboard is available when typing is required.

**Optional tracing:** Each new letter also has a touch/stylus canvas with the faint Cyrillic model underneath. Tracing is freehand motor practice, not handwriting recognition or a score.

**Optional voice:** The student can hear the exact Russian model at regular or slower device-speech speed where supported. Synthetic speech is a model, not native Russian audio or pronunciation assessment. A device with no speech synthesizer can still complete letter-recognition practice; it is not reported as an audio test.

### What if the work becomes too easy?

The learner may switch to **Steady** or **Challenge** at any moment. After the first few steps, she also has the completely optional **Test ahead by 3 steps** control.

- It contains **six separate closed-book questions**, two about each of the next three micro-steps.
- All **6/6 must be answered correctly** to advance by three.
- Failure does not remove saved progress or impose a penalty.
- Passing saves the three steps as **tested** rather than **learned**; mere tapping cannot skip them.
- The check never reveals its answers before the test is finished.
- Even after passing, the learner may revisit vocabulary, handwriting and older lessons elsewhere in the Academy.

This avoids both extremes: a beginner is never forced into a huge lesson, and a quick learner is not forced through dozens of trivial repetitions.

## Existing progress and the next course

The default first-steps landing appears only to an **actual new learner**. Previous learners who already started the 210-session Today program or saved older lesson/grammar/writing progress keep their former place. New learners who start the micro-steps retain their chosen gentle/harder path if they later explore other modules.

After all 78 rungs, Today automatically offers the existing **210 guided sessions** and more complex work in vocabulary, grammar, cases, listening, reading, writing and scripted speaking. The full Academy remains voluntarily accessible from the beginning.

Micro-step position, chosen difficulty, error tally and tested/skipped step history are included in the existing **version-1 portable backup** without invalidating older backups.

## Verification and limitations

Automated tests cover 33-letter completeness, the first six absolute-beginner steps, prerequisite-safe word spelling, all 78 unique stages, 2/3/4 check difficulty modes, mandatory correct answers before completion, the optional 6/6 fast-track test, user-history migration, offline cache links, touch tracing route, and safe JSON backup validation.

This is a purposeful **pre-literacy foundation**, not a promise of CEFR A1/B2 by itself. Native speaker listening, pronunciation correction, independent free conversation and quality review of longer essays remain separate skills needed later in the curriculum. First-steps tests count coached practice; they do not certify fluency.
